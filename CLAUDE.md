# ClearFrame — Project Context

Autonomous rights-clearance department for film & TV. Google Cloud "Agentic Cinema" hackathon, **Parallel track**, deadline **Sep 10 2026, 2:30am IST**. Devpost judging: tech implementation, complete product (not PoC), credible impact, non-obvious idea.

## Architecture (read before editing)

Deterministic 13-stage pipeline: **script** (pre-scan) → **scan** (Gemini video, Vertex) → **triage** (rules+dedupe) → **corroborate** (independent logo catalogue confirms identity) → **drift** (script vs screen) → **preview** (complete footage-derived report, no research) → **research** (Parallel Task API fan-out, Basis citations = audit trail) → **freshness** (Parallel Search, live enforcement check) → **risk** (pure-code rubric in `scoring.py` — NEVER an LLM) → **territory** (per-jurisdiction bands) → **coverage** (rights ledger: are we already licensed?) → **remediation** (template drafts) → **court** → human review → **dossier** (the E&O clearance report, in HTML and DOCX). `ANALYSIS_STAGES` in `pipeline.py` is the source of truth — update it and the event-count test together.

- Core (`models.py`, `scoring.py`, `triage.py`, `pipeline.py`, `corroboration.py`, `territory.py`, `freshness.py`, `licensing.py`, `matching.py`, `routing.py`, `knowledge.py`, `audio.py`, `exporters/`) must not import `google.*`.
- **`routing.py` is the escalation ladder** — the module that decides *how much to spend* on each finding, and the reason a run is minutes rather than tens of minutes. LOCAL (rights table, 0ms/$0) → STATUTE (settled law, 0ms/$0) → SEARCH (Parallel Search, ~2s/$0.005) → DEEP (Parallel Task, minutes). A rung is only correct if it can answer the question its category poses: trademark ownership is a local lookup so SEARCH suffices, artwork ownership IS the question so art goes DEEP. Anything at or above the HIGH band escalates regardless of category — cheapest-that-can-answer is the right default, not the right rule for the findings that sink a delivery.
- **`knowledge.py` + `data/rights/`** — 161 marks, 18 cases, 18 counterparties, US term rules. `cases_for()` feeds `liability.py` (injunction risk) and, since Phase 11, `routing.py` (tattoos answer from the record instead of the open web). Ownership is asserted locally (a corporate fact); **posture deliberately is not**, unless a documented citable action exists, because whether a holder is suing people this quarter is exactly what a static table cannot know. That is what keeps Parallel load-bearing on every material finding.
- **`audio.py`** — music identity is *measured*, not inferred. A generic label ("Upbeat electronic music") is an absence of identity, so a fingerprint hit PROMOTES it; a specific label that disagrees is CONFLICTED and blocks research as usual.
- **`use_context` and `depiction` are the two variables that actually decide trademark risk.** Rogers v. Grimaldi protects expressive works and not advertising (narrowed further by Jack Daniel's v. VIP, 2023), and the litigation record splits cleanly on it: every brand-owner *win* is an advert (Falkner v. GM, Mercedes v. the Detroit muralists, Revok v. H&M), every *loss* is an expressive work (Wham-O, Caterpillar, Louis Vuitton). Rights holders then object to *portrayal*, not presence — NBC digitally erased In-Sink-Erator from *Heroes* only because a hand got mangled in it. Both default to the calibration baseline (`EXPRESSIVE`, `depiction=None`), so a production that declares nothing scores exactly as it did before they existed. **Music is exempt from both**: sync + master are required whichever medium it is.
- **`platform.py` scores DETECTABILITY, not legal merit — and they invert.** A background mural carries real legal weight and is almost never caught; a 12-second music bed has an excellent fair-use argument and is caught essentially always, because [Content ID does not evaluate fair use](https://torrentfreak.com/youtube-processed-2-5-billion-content-id-copyright-claims-in-2025/) (2.5B claims in 2025, 99%+ automated, 90%+ monetised rather than removed). Default platform is `none` (theatrical/festival), which is honest: no automated enforcement exists there, so clearance matters *more*, not less.
- **`territory.py` answers "which law governs this finding here", not just panorama.** 12 jurisdictions × 8 dimensions in `data/jurisdictions.json`. The ones that change advice: parody (statutory in GB/DE/FR/ES, fair dealing in CA/AU, fair use in the US — **absent in India, Japan, Italy**), open vs closed exceptions (only the US is open, so a fair-use memo does not travel), incidental inclusion (India s.52(1)(u) is unusually generous *for artistic works only, not music*), moral rights (perpetual in FR/BR/ES/IT). `governing_regime()` fixes a quiet US-centrism: the same background face is GDPR in the EU, DPDP in India, LGPD in Brazil. **`assess()` picks the dimension that actually governs the finding.** It used to read 2 of the 8 and run the whole COPYRIGHT_ART category through the panorama branch, so a face tattoo, a t-shirt print and an indoor sticker were banded under the exception for public sculpture and cited to it — and it moved the band, down in DE/IN and up in FR. Panorama now requires `siting == "public_permanent"` on an ARTWORK or LOCATION; a non-sited copyright work is governed by **incidental inclusion** instead; marks get the expressive-use question and are told a US Rogers memo does not travel to a closed-list jurisdiction; music is told plainly that sync and master are required everywhere; perpetual moral rights are surfaced in FR/BR/ES/IT.
- **`sourcework.py` answers "what IS this footage", not "what is in it"** — and the medium decides everything. In a DRAWN work the studio authored every character, prop and background, so an element of the work really is covered by a licence to the work: a Code Geass clip produced 7 deep runs into 5 characters of 1 work with 1 rights holder, 6 returning nothing. Subsuming them took it to 0. **Live action inverts this** — the camera photographs a world full of other people's property, which is the entire reason clearance departments exist. `SourceWork.medium` gates it and `unknown` subsumes nothing: silence is not permission to suppress a finding. Never subsumed in any medium: real brands (Pizza Hut paid for that placement and the sponsorship did not travel), music (sync + master are separate paper), real people (a release is signed by them, not the studio).
- **`liability.py` turns a band into money** — headline, clear-now vs fix-in-post, statutory range (**copyright only**: 17 U.S.C. §504(c), $750–$30,000, $150,000 willful — trademark has no statutory damages), injunction risk read from `litigation.json`, and a 3-line escalation. `_fix_line()` is category-aware because you do not VFX paint-out a song. The injunction column is the point: brand owners mostly *lose* trademark depiction cases yet productions still pay, because Woods v. Universal got a preliminary injunction against *12 Monkeys* **while it was in theatres**. The cost is the release date, not the judgment.
- **`overlay.py` decides which box to draw and when** — mirrored exactly in `VideoPlayer.tsx` `boxAt()`. Per-appearance box first (`TimeRange.bbox`), then the legacy `at_s` window, then the legacy element range. `TOLERANCE_S = 2.0`. **`locates()` is the last word on all of them**: a box covering ≥`MAX_LOCATING_AREA` (0.9) of the frame is dropped, because it restates that the element is in the shot — which the finding already said — and paints over every accurate box beneath it. It is also required before two sightings can be called colocated in `triage`, since a whole-frame rectangle overlaps every other rectangle in the picture — and before a detector hit can be called the same OBJECT as a finding in `corroboration.leftover_hits`. `bind_ground_boxes` lives here too: grounding answers per LABEL and the player draws per FINDING, so one claimant takes every rectangle for its label while several claimants take at most one each, paired to whichever is nearest their own scan box. Two findings are never handed the same rectangle. Boxes are drawn **only while paused**: an honesty decision, not a tidiness one — a box that lags a moving object reads as a wrong detection.
- **`media.py` is the only module that reads a video file directly** (ffmpeg via `imageio-ffmpeg`, no third-party service). It exists because every upload declared `duration_s = 0.0`, so `audio.sample_offsets` took ONE fingerprint sample at the head of a 49s clip instead of four across it.
- **`triage.merge_appearances` is the other half of `overlay.py`'s rule.** The scan splits a moving element into per-shot appearances each carrying its own rectangle; merging them by interval union kept the earliest box and widened the span, which paints shot 1's rectangle across shot 4 — the exact thing the overlay refuses to draw. Merging is by COVERAGE: finest measurement of any second wins, a fully covered range still donates its box to a survivor that has none, and only a boxless pair may weld. Where two boxes compete the SHORTER range's wins (less subject travel folded into a union box). `MIN_SPLIT_S` (0.25s) stops boundary noise becoming a sub-frame range that `timeline` would then disown, costing the element every box it had. `union_spans` keeps the old boxless weld for unscanned ranges. **Any change here must be replayed through real detector output** — the output tiles the covered set, so screen time is unchanged, and that invariant is what the replay checks.
- **`timeline.py` asks whether the scan's timecodes are POSSIBLE, not whether they are right.** Physical tests only, so no threshold needs defending: an appearance shorter than one frame was never photographed, and neither was one after the last frame. It deliberately does NOT repair the unit — minutes (×60) and fraction-of-clip both landed inside the clip on the case that found it, so choosing would be guessing, and `overlay.py`'s rule is that a confidently wrong rectangle is worse than none. The finding keeps its band and its cost; only its timing is disowned, and the player says so where the box would have been.
- **Frame grounding (`media.extract_frame` + `gemini_client.parse_ground_payload` + `GET /api/productions/{pid}/ground`)** measures the box on the frame you actually paused on. It exists because three separate box defects all traced to one cause: Gemini samples video at ~1fps and returns ONE rectangle per time range, a union of where the subject travelled rather than where it is in any frame. A still has none of that. **`parse_ground_payload` drops any label the scan did not already find** — grounding LOCATES, it does not detect, and a box for an element that never went through triage, corroboration and routing would be a second unaudited detector. Cached per whole second **and per media_version** — the old `(pid, second)` key served a second upload the first film's rectangles as measured truth — only asked about elements the analysis says are on screen, one entry per distinct label, fixture twin for demo mode. Returns a LIST of boxes per element: one mark can be on the cap and on the box in the same frame. **Once a frame is grounded its answer is the whole answer**: falling back to the scan's box when grounding says "not here" puts the video guess back precisely where it was checked and rejected.
- **`exposure.py` is the only module about things nobody owns.** Addresses, documents, screens, plates, minors. Kept entirely out of the clearance element list. MINOR is the ceiling in every territory and is deliberately not scaled by regime — scaling it would imply somewhere that publishing a child's face is more acceptable.
- **`grounding.py` is the half of the anchor-box path that crosses a container boundary.** The measuring used to live in `create_app`'s closure, and the trigger in `create_production`'s — both of which run only under `InProcessJobQueue`. So pre-grounding worked on a laptop and silently did **not** work in the deployed split topology, for two independent reasons and with no error from either: the worker's listener had no hook, and `_ground_cache`/`_pregrounded`/`_preground_progress` were in-memory dicts the worker could not reach. Measured on the deployed site: **13.98s cold, 0.19s warm, and nothing in the bucket**; locally the same call is 11-14ms. `GroundStore` is modelled on `EventLog` — the precedent for "worker writes, API reads" — but writes **one blob per second** rather than one per production, because four measurements are in flight at once and a read-modify-write against a single object loses answers. `media_version` is IN the key path, a lesson already paid for twice. `_ground_second` is now three tiers — memory → blob → Gemini — and **a blob MISS falls through to measuring, never to `grounded: false`**; collapsing that distinction would suppress every box on the frame for ever. The worker's warm-up is **awaited before the request returns**, because Cloud Run throttles CPU once a response is sent. A second is measured at the MIDPOINT of the part actually covered by an appearance (`measure_instant`), not at its first frame: one rectangle answers for the whole second and is marked `grounded: true`, so measuring at `N.0` showed a reviewer paused at 32.9s a box measured at 32.0s — a full second of movement, presented as measured truth. The midpoint halves worst case and average for the same money; clamping matters because a naive `N + 0.5` measures an instant a 4.0-4.2s appearance has already left.
- **`own_content` is the second thing that takes a rectangle away** (the first is a disowned clock). The scan reported a film's own subtitles as a finding — `TEXT / "On-screen subtitles"`, **17 boxed appearances** over a 41.5s clip — and the player drew every one, on top of the words they described. `routing._route_text` already called `is_own_content`, landed on STATUTE and wrote *"Own work — no third-party right implicated" / "No action. Recorded so the dossier is complete, not because it is a risk."* The system reached the right conclusion and drew the opposite of it. Decided once in `triage` (the last point before `plan_seconds` reads a box), honoured in `overlay.box_at`, `grounding.elements_at`/`plan_seconds` and the client's `boxesAt`. `visible_at` is deliberately unchanged — the finding stays listed, because that is what its own disposition promises. **Scoped to TEXT_ON_SCREEN exactly as `_route_text` scopes it**: the vocabulary holds `lower`, `third`, `super` and `bug`, so widening it would strip the box off a LOCATION called "Lower East Side".
- **`reportmeta.py` holds the document furniture both report formats share** — reference number, status, control rows, signatories, confidentiality legend. Two renderers computing them separately is how they drift. `media_version` is in the control block deliberately: it is the only thing that names WHICH CUT was reviewed.
- **The dossier is one document in two formats — `dossier.html` and `dossier.docx`, and nothing else.** It used to be five equal filename links including a JSON dump and three spreadsheets. `exporters/edl.py`, `csv_markers.py`, `cue_sheet.py` and `safe_cell` were **deleted** on 2026-09-07 at the user's explicit instruction; the loss is real and worth remembering — the EDL imported into DaVinci Resolve as timeline markers, and the cue sheet was the literal ASCAP/BMI submission format. `python-docx` is a **base** dependency, not an extra, because the Word file is one of only two artifacts.
- **`cast.py` is the one module that takes findings AWAY.** A face the scan attributes to a named performer (`"Phil Wenneck (Bradley Cooper)"`, or "played by actor Ed Helms" in the description) is not a third-party finding: you cannot have Bradley Cooper playing Phil Wenneck without a contract with Bradley Cooper. It becomes a `CastCredit` — no box, no route, no band — printed once with the guild obligation. The boundary is the design and it is **asymmetric on purpose**: cast needs POSITIVE evidence, so an unnamed person stays a finding, because an extra in a doorway does need a release and is personal data under GDPR/DPDP/LGPD besides. A name alone is not evidence — a documentary subject has a name and has signed nothing. Faces only; drawn characters belong to `sourcework.subsumed_by`, and two modules answering one question is how a tattoo got two verdicts.
- **`conflicts.py` is the one check that is not about infringement.** Category exclusivity is standard in sponsorship contracts, so a rival mark in shot can void the fee even though the depiction is lawful. Deliberately conservative — uncatalogued marks are left alone, because a false conflict costs the user a real conversation with a sponsor.
- Identity verdicts, territory bands and coverage states are deterministic like risk scores — LLMs and detectors observe, pure code decides. A `CONFLICTED` identity blocks research; corroborator silence is `SINGLE_SOURCE`, never a conflict; coverage is `UNKNOWN` (not `NOT_COVERED`) whenever ownership or identity is unresolved.
- `scan` runs **three** independent Gemini passes (`CLEARFRAME_SCAN_PASSES`), Cloud Video Intelligence and acoustic fingerprinting **concurrently** — none depends on another's output, so running them in sequence paid their latencies added together. Results are stashed on the state; `corroborate` consumes them and falls back to calling the detector itself if the prefetch is absent. `detector_checked` distinguishes an *empty* prefetch from an absent one; before it, `if not hits` could not, and Video Intelligence was called a second time for zero extra signal — 65s per live run. Same shape as the AudD bug `audio_checked` fixed. **Fixed Phase 11**; this bullet said "BUG (open)" for a day after it was closed, which is its own lesson.
- `<out>/state/` holds production JSON **and** `licences.json`. Never `glob("*.json")` it — use `LocalJsonStore.production_ids()`.
- Integration clients sit behind protocols with **fixture** and **live** implementations sharing one parser — demo mode (zero creds/network) exercises the identical code path. Fixtures: `src/clearframe/integrations/fixtures/`.
- ADK wrapper: `src/clearframe/adk/agents.py` (`SequentialAgent` of custom `StageAgent`s, google-adk 2.7).
- Review app: `src/clearframe/webapp/server.py` (FastAPI, server-side role gating, `asyncio.Lock` serializes all state saves) + `webapp/` React SPA (**prebuilt `webapp/dist` is committed** — rebuild with `npm run build` after any webapp/src change and commit dist).
- **The landing screen is a dashboard, and each production's poster is a FRAME OF ITS OWN FOOTAGE** (`GET /api/productions/{pid}/thumbnail`). The moment is the middle of the first finding's first appearance — what the analysis says the footage is about — cached on disk under `media_version` and purged with the other per-upload caches; 404 when there is nothing to extract, so the fallback art is drawn in the client. **Do not put studio posters or stock film art in this repo**: it is a rights-clearance product that already carries a pre-submission task to purge a third-party clip from its own history, and decorating it with other people's marketing images is the one joke it cannot afford. This was offered and declined on 2026-09-06.
- **`webapp/auth.py` is the gate, and it is OPT-IN.** `CLEARFRAME_AUTH=firebase` turns it on; unset, the app behaves exactly as before — which is what keeps a credential-free suite and smoke script green and the public demo reachable by a judge with no account. Enforced as ONE middleware, not a dependency on seventeen routes, because the routes that most needed guarding were the ones nobody had decorated: before this, the two CHEAP mutations were role-gated and every EXPENSIVE one was open (the upload that runs 3 Gemini passes + 25 Parallel runs, 150 grounding calls, freshness, the dossier's monitors). The role was a header the browser set — `curl -H 'X-ClearFrame-Role: legal'` was a complete bypass, and `smoke.sh` still uses it as its happy path in demo mode. Role now resolves custom claim → `CLEARFRAME_ROLE_MAP` allowlist → **`editor`** (least privilege: signed in ≠ counsel). **The token rides in an HttpOnly cookie, not a header**, because `EventSource`, `<video src>` and the dossier `<a href>` cannot send one. Deliberately NOT `create_session_cookie` — it needs Identity Toolkit signing rights that may not exist under a user-account ADC. `verify_token` is the only function that touches Google, so all 19 auth tests run offline. `firebase-admin` is an optional `[auth]` extra, imported lazily: the demo image installs base deps only and has zero Google packages in it.
- **`CLEARFRAME_OPEN_ROLES=1` lets a signed-in visitor CHOOSE their role, and exists for one reason: a judge opening the deployed URL cannot email the author and wait to be made `legal`.** An app that shows a stranger a read-only view of every interesting control has not been evaluated. It is a MODE and never a default — off, the role comes from the verified user and the header is not evidence; on, the sign-in screen offers a role picker and the header is honoured *because self-selection is the point, so there is nothing to bypass*. Two things do not change when it is on: **you still have to sign in**, and the decision is still recorded against the verified email of whoever pressed the button. Choosing a role is not inventing a person. The UI labels it (`chosen` chip in the header, a note on the picker) because a self-selected role presented as governance is worse than no governance.
- **Token verification does not check revocation.** Signature, issuer, audience and expiry only. A user deleted in the Firebase console keeps access until their token expires — up to an hour — and this was observed directly: a browser holding a just-deleted account's cookie walked past the gate. `check_revoked=True` costs a network round trip on every request, and the middleware runs on every call including media and grounding. Documented rather than fixed; if it ever matters, the answer is revocation checking plus a short cache.
- **The audit trail names a person now.** `reviewer` was the role WORD, so an E&O dossier recorded that *"legal"* signed off a clearance decision. `Decision.reviewer` and `AuditEvent.actor` were already free-form strings, so a verified email dropped in with no migration. This is the product win, not the gate.
- **`POST /api/webhooks/parallel-monitor` takes a shared secret** (`CLEARFRAME_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, `hmac.compare_digest`). It writes to the audit trail and can flip a signed-off dossier back to `awaiting`, and the monitor ids it keys on are readable from an open endpoint. Unset leaves it open, which is the old behaviour and what keeps smoke green.

## Working constraints (standing — ask before breaking any of these)

Pushing there is fine. **The repo goes public only as a deliberate submission step**, and two things must be settled first — see the footage-purge item in NOT DONE, and the GCP project id in `CLAUDE.md` line ~274.

- **`.env` holds live credentials and must stay gitignored.** Verify with `git check-ignore -q .env`. A file named `env` (no dot) is NOT ignored and has already leaked a live Parallel key into `git status` once.

- Update `CLAUDE.md` after every successful test cycle — the user asks for this explicitly.

- Feature branches merged to `main` after the full suite passes twice (pre- and post-merge). `pytest` must stay green with dev-only install too (cloud-dependent tests use `pytest.importorskip`).
- Risk scores must stay reproducible from stored inputs.
- Timestamps come from callers/CLI, never inside builders (`as_of` is threaded into `licensing.assess`).

- `scripts/smoke.sh` must stay green. Use its `has <needle> <curl args…>` helper for new checks — a bare `curl … | grep -q` races under `set -o pipefail` (grep exits first, curl dies of SIGPIPE 141).

## Status vs hackathon resources guide (updated 2026-08-23)

### UI-mode gotchas (found by opening the app, not by the suite)

Every one of these passed a green test suite. They live in the gap between "the
code does what I wrote" and "the thing on screen is right", and the only way
they surfaced was a human clicking.

- **`scripts/smoke.sh` used to `pkill -f "clearframe serve"`** — killing every ClearFrame server on the machine, including a dev server on another port, twice per run (the `trap cleanup EXIT` fires too). It now kills only what holds its own `$PORT`. **Never widen that pkill again.**
- **Gemini transposed the bbox axes.** The schema asked for four ANONYMOUS numbers and the prompt said `[ymin, xmin, ymax, xmax]`; the model answered x-first, the commoner convention. Word order in a prompt is not a contract — `bbox` is now an OBJECT with named edges. Diagnose box problems by drawing the stored rectangle onto a real frame with `ffmpeg drawbox`, never by reasoning.
- **One box was drawn across every appearance.** An element with four time ranges had a single rectangle, so the box measured in shot 1 was painted onto shot 4. The scan now returns `at_s` and the player draws only within 2s of it.
- **Every upload 500'd on a `NameError` that 392 tests passed over.** All footage tests bail out BEFORE the Production is constructed (409 demo / 415 type / 503 creds), so the success path — the one a user takes — was never executed. If you touch `create_production`, run an actual upload.
- **A page refresh appeared to destroy the analysis.** It never did: the pipeline is a server-side asyncio task and persists after every stage. `/api/productions` returned store order, so `demo` sorted ahead of the user's upload and the client restored the wrong one. Now newest-first with a `running` flag.
- **The rule was written down and never encoded — three times.** `territory.py` printed "Risk steps down IF this work is permanently installed and filmed from a place accessible to the public" and never tested it; the same file documented four `panorama` values in a header comment and branched on two, so Italy's `none` fell into the `buildings_only` text and a mural bound for Italy was told Italy grants an exception it does not have; `models.BBox` documented "the parser divides by 1000" and no parser did. **Treat a condition stated in prose as unimplemented until you find the branch.**
- **Gemini reported six appearances totalling 0.15s for a 10-second tattoo** — each 20-30ms, shorter than one frame at 24fps. The analysis was right and the player looked broken: timecodes in the list, no box at any instant a hand can pause on. Rare (1 bad run in 9) and non-deterministic, so it cannot be tuned away — see `timeline.py`. Diagnose by comparing each element's range total against its own `screen_time_s`: seven of eight reconciled at 1.0×, the eighth was 66.7× out.
- **A "Ray-Ban Aviator Sunglasses" box was drawn across a bare forehead, and the model was not at fault.** A control asking it to place the Eiffel Tower and the Golden Gate Bridge in the same frame returned neither. The player fell through to the scan's stored box whenever grounding omitted an element — putting the video-pass guess back on screen exactly where it had been checked and rejected. **Grounded-and-absent, grounded-and-found, and never-grounded are three answers; only the third may fall back.**
- **A second independent scan pass immediately produced duplicate findings that disagreed with each other.** "Stu's Face Tattoo" (DEEP, description said *replicating* Mike Tyson's tattoo) and "Face Tattoo" (STATUTE, plainer description) were one object with two routes and two answers to the most consequential question in the clip. `triage` grouped on exact `(type, label)` equality, which held only because the auditor is primed with the first pass's labels and echoes them. Grouping is now same-type + `labels_match`, and the fullest label and description win — **routing reads the description**.
- **Two Gemini passes both number their findings from 1.** The auditor is a *second* scan whose entire purpose is to catch what the first missed, so the moment it succeeds two unrelated findings share an id — and `routes`, `research`, `risk`, `coverage` are all dicts keyed by it. On the Hangover clip, 8 findings collapsed to 6 routes: `Stu's Face Tattoo` and a `National` car-rental logo were both id `"1"`, and **the tattoo was displayed with the car-rental company's owner, route and risk band (LOW, 8)**. Silently — nothing raised, nothing logged, the counts still looked plausible. Fixed by `stages/scan.merge_passes()`, which renames only *later* collisions (so first-pass ids and stored states hold) and runs *before* triage's `(type, label)` dedupe (so a genuine second sighting still merges).
- **The player replayed the previous clip under the new clip's boxes.** Every upload lands at the same production id, so `/api/productions/upload/media` is byte-identical between two different films and the browser does exactly what it was told. Nothing server-side is wrong when this happens, which is what makes it hard to see: state right, file on disk right, `curl` right — only a browser that already holds the URL is wrong. Fixed with `Production.media_version` (size + **nanosecond** mtime; whole seconds collide when two uploads land in the same second) in the query string, plus `Cache-Control: no-cache` on the media response.
- **Three bugs, one cause: nothing was wrong with the coordinates.** A user reported boxes that "still don't
  overlay perfectly". Drawing the stored rectangles onto real frames with `ffmpeg drawbox` — this repo's own
  rule — showed grounding placing the *National* logo exactly on the lettering of a water heater and the IWC
  box exactly on a watch face, at 720x1280, through the same normalisation and the same overlay. What was
  wrong was one class of ANSWER: asked to place a named person, the model returns `{0,0,1,1}`, the whole
  frame, in the same frames where it places a wristwatch to the pixel. So a rectangle around the entire video
  sat on top of every accurate box with its label pinned at 0%,0%. **Removing the cast fixed most of what read
  as misalignment**, and `locates()` is the backstop. Residual, and honest: a grounded box varies run to run —
  the same tattoo measured `{.118,.581,.558,.930}` at 6s on one call and `{.146,.652,.511,.842}` at 7s on
  another. The second is squarely on the tattoo; the first is loose. That is model precision on a diffuse
  subject, not a code defect, and no code change fixes it.
- **A green suite is not a replay.** The dedupe fix passed 552 tests and then deleted Ed Helms. Replaying the

### Live-mode gotchas (hard-won — read before debugging live mode)

- **`gcloud auth login` ≠ `gcloud auth application-default login`.** The Python SDK uses ADC only. On 2026-08-22 ADC was authenticated as a *different Google account* (`sumitgeorgian3986@`) with zero IAM on the project, while `gcloud config` showed the owner — every Vertex call 403'd with "Permission 'aiplatform.locations.list' denied". Check identity with `curl "https://www.googleapis.com/oauth2/v3/tokeninfo?access_token=$(gcloud auth application-default print-access-token)"`. Also set `gcloud auth application-default set-quota-project <PROJECT>`.
- **zsh eats `$VAR:` as a history modifier.** `"$M:generateContent"` expands to garbage (`5-pronerateContent`) and Google returns an **HTML 404 page** that reads exactly like "model not available". Always brace: `"${M}:generateContent"`.
- **Vertex REST is `v1beta1`**, not `v1` — the google-genai SDK targets v1beta1. A `v1` URL gives the same misleading HTML 404.
- **`gemini-3-pro-preview` genuinely 404s in this project** (clean JSON `NOT_FOUND` under correct auth, re-confirmed 2026-08-22). The coded fallback to `gemini-2.5-pro` is load-bearing: live runs are actually served by 2.5-pro.
- **Gemini video is the CHEAP option**: 258 tokens/second @ $1.25/M ≈ **$0.019/min**. A 90-min feature is ~$1.74/pass; Cloud Video Intelligence `LOGO_RECOGNITION` is $0.15/min (**7.8× more**). Never "optimise" the scan away from Gemini on cost grounds.
- **Parallel list prices** (docs.parallel.ai/getting-started/pricing, checked 2026-08-22): Search **$0.005/req**; Task per run lite $0.005 / base $0.010 / pro $0.100 / ultra $0.300. `planner.EST_COST` must track these — it was 10× high and Mission Control announced "$4.70" for a ~$0.40 run.
- **Live runs found five defects that no fixture could.** Re-read before trusting a green suite: a legal suffix defeated identity agreement (`{bayer,aspirin}` vs `{bayer,corporation}` = 0.5, under threshold) so a correctly identified brand was falsely CONFLICTED and blocked from research; useful articles ("Flower Vase") were sent to deep research; people labelled by role ("Father", "Grandmother") were researched while "Young Boy" correctly got a release form; music was researched from its description; and a mid-upload TCP reset killed an entire run. **No fixture could produce any of these** — they need real detector output and a real network.
- **`matching` has three matchers, not one.** `labels_match` (general), `marks_match` (brand identity — discounts legal suffixes only, so "Warner Music" ≠ "Warner Bros."), `holders_match` (rights holders — also discounts industry words, because half the music business shares them). The wrong one gives either a false conflict that blocks research on a correct answer, or a false licence match that tells a producer they are covered when they are not.
- **Retry transport failures, never deterministic ones** (`gemini_live._is_transient`). A 4xx, a safety block or an unknown model fails identically on retry, and the footage upload is not cheap.
- **`grep -q` takes SIGPIPE even from a buffered `printf`.** The smoke `has` helper was "fixed" once by buffering the body before piping — but `grep -q` stops reading at the first match, so any body over the 64KB pipe buffer still killed the writer. The dossier crossed that line and a passing check reported failure. Use a here-string (`grep -q -- "$needle" <<<"$body"`) — file-backed, no pipe, no race at any size.
- **Token-overlap matching produces confident false positives on company names.** "Kobalt Music Group" matched an owner string containing "Universal Music Group" on `{music, group}` alone — 2 of 3 tokens, over threshold — and reported a festival-only cue licence as covering a Weeknd master. `matching.holders_match` strips corporate furniture first. Being wrong in the *covered* direction is the one failure the ledger must not have.
- **ffmpeg now ships with the package** via `imageio-ffmpeg` (static binary, no brew, works on Cloud Run). Get the path with `imageio_ffmpeg.get_ffmpeg_exe()`, never a bare `ffmpeg`.
- **AudD returns error 900 for a well-formed token on an inactive account** — a trial must be activated in the dashboard. `api_token=test` gives 10 req/day for smoke-testing.
- **`.env` needs the dot.** A file named `env` is not gitignored; it showed as untracked with the live Parallel key in it. Verify with `git check-ignore -q .env`.

## Case coverage — the lawsuits this product is calibrated against

The rubric is not invented. Every weight traces to a real action, and the split
below is the single most load-bearing fact in `routing.py` and `scoring.py`:
**brand owners mostly LOSE against expressive works; copyright holders WIN.**
Risk is therefore *inverted* relative to where detection effort naturally goes.

| Case | What was in shot | Outcome | Where it lives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Whitmill v. Warner Bros.** (2011) | Mike Tyson's tattoo replicated on an actor's face in *The Hangover Part II* | settled; **preliminary-injunction motion weeks before release** | `litigation.json`; drove `SourceWork.medium` |
| **Woods v. Universal** (1996) | set design in *12 Monkeys* | **injunction granted while in theatres**, settled high six figures | `liability.py` injunction risk |
| **Ringgold v. BET** (1997) | a story quilt poster on a set wall, ~27s | infringement; de minimis rejected | scoring, Court fixtures |
| **Sandoval v. New Line** (1998) | photographs, out of focus, ~35s | de minimis **succeeded** | the other side of the same line |
| **Rogers v. Grimaldi** (1989) | title/persona in an expressive work | speech wins | `use_context` |
| **Jack Daniel's v. VIP** (2023) | — | narrowed Rogers | `use_context` |
| **Caterpillar v. Disney**, **Wham-O v. Paramount**, **Louis Vuitton v. WB** | branded goods in films | brand owner **lost** each time | why trademark defaults to cheap rungs |
| **Falkner v. GM**, **Mercedes v. Detroit muralists**, **Revok v. H&M** | murals in **adverts** | brand/artist **won** | why `ADVERTISING` scores 1.4× |
| **In-Sink-Erator / NBC *Heroes*** | garbage disposal mangles a hand | mark digitally erased in post, no suit | `DepictionTone` — portrayal, not presence |
| **Solid Oak v. 2K** (2020) | tattoos on NBA avatars | studio won (de minimis + implied licence) | the counterweight to Whitmill |
| **WKRP in Cincinnati** | music licensed for broadcast only | re-release blocked for decades | `licensing.py` territory/term/media gaps |

Known blind spots, stated honestly: no PD-status check on old works beyond
`public_domain.json`; no chain-of-title verification; posture is deliberately
never asserted locally (that is what keeps Parallel load-bearing).
