A de minimis defense is likely to fail because the artwork is integral to the plot, making its use legally cognizable despite the brief screen time. The strongest argument rests on a four-factor fair use analysis: 1. **Purpose and Character of the Use:** This factor strongly favors fair use. The use is transformative. 'Zero's Mask' is not merely displayed for its intrinsic artistic value; it is recontextualized as a key narrative device within a new expressive work ('Untitled Production'). Its purpose is transformed from art into a plot point, which is a classic transformative use. While the production is commercial, transformativeness is the key inquiry and weighs heavily in our favor. 2. **Nature of the Copyrighted Work:** The mask is a work of creative art, placing it at the core of copyright protection. This factor weighs against fair use, but can be overcome by other factors. 3. **Amount and Substantiality Used:** The quantitative use is minimal—only 1.5 seconds of screen time and 10% of the frame. Qualitatively, while the whole mask might be seen, it is used only to the extent necessary to fulfill its narrative function. The use is not an attempt to exhibit the art, but to advance the story, which supports a finding of fairness. 4. **Effect on the Potential Market:** The use does not usurp the market for the original artwork. An audience viewing 'Untitled Production' will not consider it a substitute for purchasing or viewing the actual 'Zero's Mask.' While the use might theoretically impact a licensing market, this harm is speculative and considered less significant by courts when the use is highly transformative. The production's good-faith, albeit unsuccessful, search for the rights holder also underscores the lack of willful infringement and strengthens the overall equitable position.
